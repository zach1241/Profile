Organic leads start with the page, not the keyword list

Keyword research tells us where demand exists. The page still has to earn trust and make the next step easy.

By Zachary Swanepoel14 October 20264 min read

Keyword research tells us where demand exists. The page still has to earn trust and make the next step easy.

A keyword is not a complete brief

A keyword list might tell us that people search for a service in a particular area. It does not explain everything a visitor expects to see after clicking. For a local service business, visitors may be looking for coverage areas, proof of experience, response times or a direct way to call.

I find it more useful to treat the keyword as the beginning of a page brief. What question is being asked, what evidence can the business provide, and what action should the visitor take next?

Match content to the real decision

The basics still matter: clear headings, descriptive page titles, logical links and content written for the topic. But the page also needs to behave well. A broken form or hidden phone number can erase the value of strong organic visibility.

If the page is not helping somebody decide, more words and more keywords will not automatically make it better.

Personal perspective

My take

SEO and conversion-focused design belong in the same conversation. I would rather create a handful of pages that each serve a real purpose than publish dozens of near-duplicates for the sake of coverage.
